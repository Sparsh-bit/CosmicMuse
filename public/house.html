The House

We Don't Make
Perfume.

We make memories. A modern house, built on ancient traditions, working at a scale that lets us know every material by name.

Beginnings

It started with
a mistake.

A saffron tincture left too long in aged oak. Six months later it had turned the colour of dark honey and smelled of a room someone had just left. That accident became No. 01, and it took four years to learn how to repeat it on purpose.

Everything since has been built the same way — slowly, from materials we can trace, in quantities small enough that one person can smell every batch.

Sourcing

Traceable, or
not in the bottle.

We buy at origin wherever it is possible, which is most of the time. Where it is not — some materials simply pass through too many hands — we say so on the product page rather than dressing it up.

What we will not do

A short list.

We do not use wild-harvested agarwood. We do not reformulate quietly — if a material becomes unavailable and a fragrance changes, we say so and change the batch number. We do not run sales; the price is the price, all year. And we do not make anything we would not wear ourselves, which is the only rule that has ever mattered.
